# Colour

Colour here is two layers, and keeping them apart is the entire point.

A **primitive** is a value with no meaning. `n-600` is a grey, nothing more. There are
{Object.keys(palette).length} of them, they are generated from an OKLCH design intent and verified
in the sRGB gamut, and no component may reference one directly.

A **role** is a decision. `content-muted` says "this text is secondary but still meant to be read",
and it happens to resolve to `n-600` today. There are {Object.keys(lightColors).length} roles, and
they are the only names a component is allowed to use, through `t('content-muted')`.

The reason for the split is regression, not tidiness. The app this replaces had 67 hardcoded colour
literals and no role layer, so every theme change was a search and replace, and every search and
replace missed something. With a role layer, a theme is a set of role definitions and a component
does not change at all.

**Dark mode only ever redefines roles.** Primitives never move. Both maps below are keyed
identically ({Object.keys(darkColors).length} roles each), which is what makes "the same product
with the lamp off" a checkable claim rather than a hope. Dark is not inverted light: the surface
ladder steps 4 to 5 lightness points instead of 2.5, because a shadow does almost nothing on a dark
ground and the ladder has to carry depth on its own. Status fills are the new low-chroma `-900`
steps rather than the light theme's tints, because `danger-50` inverted naively lands on a
mid-saturation red that shouts louder on black than the row title it is annotating.

### The brand, which is not an accent

One role, one element: the Melonade mark in the nav. It is watermelon, taken off the live landing
page, and in Graphite it is the only chromatic thing in the entire interface.

That is not a hole in the monochrome rule, it is the strongest reading of it. The argument this
option makes is that colour should be rationed until it means something. The primary action is ink,
the accent is one restrained teal held for state, and the logo is neither of those things: it means
*Melonade* and nothing else, which is exactly why it earns the one saturated glyph on screen.

`brand-mark` is deliberately not wired into any semantic role. If it ever appears on a control, that
is a bug. A logo is identity. An accent is a signal.
