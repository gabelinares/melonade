# Space, shape and layout

## Space

A 4pt base with two half steps below it, and a longer top end than a dense table needs, because this
option buys its calm with space rather than with borders. Every bar below is drawn at its real width,
so the gap between adjacent steps is visible rather than asserted: `space-1` through `space-4` are the
inside of a control, `space-5` and `space-6` are the inside of a pane, and everything from `space-8`
up is between blocks.

`space-px` exists so a 1px gap in a flex column is a token rather than a literal, which is what keeps
the divided lists in the filter popovers from drifting to 2px one at a time.

## Radius

Radius encodes size, not taste: the smaller the thing, the tighter the corner, so a chip inside a
menu never looks rounder than the menu holding it. Every step here is larger than the equivalent in
the denser option, and that is the cheapest legible difference between the two designs at a glance.

## Border width

<BorderWidths />

There is no 2px rule in this system, and the second token exists to say so out loud. Emphasis comes
from the border colour stepping up the neutral ramp, `border-subtle` to `border-default` to
`border-strong`, never from the line getting thicker. In this option a border is also the third
choice rather than the first: a surface change or a gap is tried before a line is drawn, which is why
the whole detail pane header contains exactly one rule.

## Layout

These are not spacing. They are the four or five numbers that make this layout this layout, so they
are tokens and they are drawn here at real width.

`rail-width` is the load-bearing one. The rail is 56px at every window size and every agent count,
which is the answer to "what happens when there are eleven agents": the eleventh costs vertical space
that was empty anyway and no width at all, ever. `list-width` is a reading measure rather than a
fraction of the window, because a queue you skim has an ideal column width and a percentage does not
know that. `row-height` is what the loading skeleton matches exactly, so the column does not resize
when data lands.
