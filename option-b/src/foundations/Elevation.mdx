# Elevation

**The rule: a card gets a border, a floating layer gets a soft wide shadow, and nothing gets a heavy
one.**

There are three shadows and a `none`, and `none` is what almost everything uses. The standing note on
the app this replaces is that its panel shadow reads as "something thick", and that is what happens
when a shadow is used to separate a card from a page: the page and the card are on the same plane, so
the shadow describes a depth that is not there. An edge does the job at 1px and costs the layout
nothing.

This option leans on that even harder than a card-based layout would, because it has almost no cards.
The rail, the list column and the detail pane are surfaces at different lightness values, not objects
sitting on a page, so depth is already carried by the colour ladder. Adding a shadow between two panes
would be saying the same thing twice and getting it wrong the second time.

A shadow is for a layer that genuinely floats, which means one that is temporary and covers content
the reader was just looking at: a popover, a menu, the capture panel, a modal. There the shadow is not
decoration, it is the only cue that the thing underneath is still there.

The shape of those two shadows is deliberate. They are **soft and wide** rather than tight and dark:
a small blur with high opacity reads as a hard edge and makes a 300px popover look like a slab, while
a wide low-opacity spread reads as air between two planes. `shadow-card` exists for the one hairline
lift and is almost invisible on purpose, so nobody reaches for `shadow-popover` to get a card to
"pop".

Both shadows compose their colour from the `shadow-color` role, which is why dark mode can flatten
them without touching a component: on a near-black ground a shadow is invisible, so the surface
ladder carries depth instead. That is also why the dark ladder steps as widely as it does.
